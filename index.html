About

I am an Associate Professor in the Division of Biomedical Informatics at Cincinnati Children's, and a Research Associate Professor in the University of Cincinnati Department of Pediatrics. I studied Information Technology at the University of Economics in Prague, and earned my PhD in Computer Science from the University of Cincinnati in 2006 under Prof. Jerome L. Paul.

My primary focus is leading informatics for large, NIH-funded data management and coordinating centers. These centers are the connective tissue of multi-site research: a single consortium may span dozens of clinical sites, thousands of participants, and years of longitudinal follow-up, and none of it becomes usable science without a platform that can capture data consistently, harmonize it across sites and instruments, enforce governance and consent, and then hand it to investigators in a form they can actually analyze. Building that platform well — and keeping it working across a decade-long grant cycle — is the problem I find most interesting.

Across the RDCRN, B2B CHANGE and LungMAP programs the recurring themes are the same: scalability; processing phenotypic and genotypic data together; interoperable metadata schemas; secure multi-institutional sharing; integrating AI; and web portals that make the resulting resource genuinely usable by researchers who did not generate the data.

Earlier in my career I spent thirteen years as Faculty Director of Research IT Services at Cincinnati Children's (2007–2020), leading a group of 22 staff who ran the computational infrastructure for the academic health center — petabyte storage, the computational cluster, private and public cloud, and virtual desktop infrastructure — and who handled the security and compliance that research computing quietly depends on. I handed day-to-day management to my team and now work on the data problems that infrastructure was built to serve.

My work is largely collaborative. I was one of the key architects of iLINCS, the integrative data analysis portal for the NIH LINCS program, and I continue to lead its development. With the Division of Behavioral Medicine and Clinical Psychology I design data and application architectures for web-based dietary interventions in cystic fibrosis, studies of online behavior in at-risk teens, and medication-adherence tooling. With marketing and psychology groups I built the technology behind survey-based Implicit Association Tests. And with the computer science department I still work on Boolean satisfiability — reconfigurable high-performance computing applied to combinatorics, where purpose-built SAT solvers on Beowulf and FPGA clusters resolved W(2, 6) = 1132, the first new two-color van der Waerden number in roughly thirty years, and later W(3, 4) = 293.

Data Management & Coordinating Centers

Cincinnati Children's hosts the coordinating center for several national NIH research consortia, and contributes infrastructure to others. I lead the informatics for these programs — the data platforms, standards and portals that turn multi-site collection into a durable, shareable scientific resource.

RDCRN

Rare Diseases Clinical Research Network

NIH NCATS · U2C TR002818 · Data Management and Coordinating Center

The RDCRN connects consortia studying rare diseases across the United States and internationally, where any single condition may have too few patients at any one site to study alone. The DMCC at Cincinnati Children's provides the network's shared clinical research infrastructure — protocol and regulatory support, electronic data capture and REDCap services, a cloud data platform, and the patient-engagement and dissemination machinery that gets findings back to the communities they concern.

My role: informatics leadership for the data management core — cloud data infrastructure, EDC systems, and cross-consortium data standards and sharing.

B2B CHANGE

Bench to Bassinet — Congenital Heart disease Advancing New understanding in GEnomics

NIH NHLBI · U01 HL131003, U01 HL184167 · Coordinating Center

B2B CHANGE is the next phase of the NIH Bench to Bassinet program and the successor to the Pediatric Cardiac Genomics Consortium. Its emphasis shifts from enrollment and genetic discovery toward longitudinal follow-up: how genetic differences shape clinical outcomes, quality of life, and future care needs. Cincinnati Children's has served as the coordinating center since 2016 and maintains the B2B DataHub (HeartsMart); the program was renewed by the NHLBI in 2026 for a further six years.

My role: informatics lead — integrating newly generated genomic and phenotypic data with the existing resource, and providing the access, tools and support that let investigators nationwide build on it.

LungMAP

Molecular Atlas of Lung Development Program

NIH NHLBI · U24 HL148865 · Data Coordination Center

LungMAP is an open-access molecular atlas of the developing and diseased lung. The Data Coordination Center at Cincinnati Children's makes the atlas interoperable — standardizing data formats and metadata schemas, and cross-validating, annotating and linking bulk and single-cell multi-omics data alongside a large collection of imaging data spanning mammalian lung development. Phase 3 extends this to AI approaches for communicating lung knowledge to the research community.

My role: building out LungMAP.net into a gateway portal connecting research networks, multi-omics datasets and image collections — described in Am J Respir Cell Mol Biol (2024).

Echinobase

Community genomics research resource for echinoderms

NIH · P41 HD095831 (University of Florida) · Model organism knowledge base

Echinobase is the international echinoderm model organism knowledge base and a sister database to Xenbase. The resource is led from the University of Florida, with substantial scientific contribution from Cincinnati Children's.

My role: setting up the on-premises datacenter resources at Cincinnati Children's to host an online copy of the database and software from the University of Calgary.

Research & Focus Areas

My work sits where research infrastructure meets applied informatics — the systems have to be built well for the science on top of them to be possible at all.

Data Coordination

Multi-site data platforms

Electronic data capture, harmonization across sites and instruments, governance and consent, and secure multi-institutional data sharing for national consortia.

Standards

Metadata & interoperability

Common data models and metadata schemas that let independently generated datasets be cross-validated, linked and reused beyond the group that produced them.

Clinical Informatics

Medication safety

Automated identification of antibiotic overdoses and adverse drug events from prescribing-alert data, and analysis of prescriber response to clinical decision support.

NLP

Clinical text & de-identification

Gold-standard corpus construction for medical NLP tasks and shareable annotated corpora for de-identification research.

Bioinformatics

Omics data platforms

Web portals and reanalysis platforms for LINCS, LungMAP, GEO RNA-seq and proteomics data — GREIN, piNET, and the LINCS Data Portal ecosystem.

Behavioral Medicine

Digital health interventions

Data and application architecture for web-based dietary interventions in cystic fibrosis, studies of online behavior in at-risk adolescents, and medication-adherence tooling.

Public Health

Real-time dashboards

An interactive dashboard tracking COVID-19 across U.S. counties, cities and states in real time, built early in the pandemic.

Combinatorics

FPGA-accelerated SAT

Exhaustive parallel search for van der Waerden numbers using custom preprocessing and SAT solvers on Beowulf and FPGA clusters.

Software & Platforms

Research platforms I have architected or helped build. Several remain in active use well beyond the groups that funded them.

iLINCS

The integrative data analysis portal for the NIH LINCS program — connecting omics signatures to biological mechanism across a very large body of perturbation data. I was one of its key architects and continue to lead its development.

Nat Commun 2022

LungMAP.net

A gateway portal into the lung atlas, linking research networks, bulk and single-cell multi-omics datasets, and a large image collection spanning mammalian lung development.

AJRCMB 2024

GREIN

An interactive web platform for re-analyzing published GEO RNA-seq data, so existing public datasets can be reprocessed and reused without rebuilding a pipeline.

Sci Rep 2019

OncMAP

An adherence processor that turns raw actuations from electronic adherence monitoring devices — eCAP, SimpleMed, MEMS, AdhereTech — into analyzable adherence data, with visualization and export for research teams.

JAMIA Open 2025

PCATS

A patient-centered shared decision-making tool used at point of care, powered by Bayesian causal inference over observational data. PCATS.JIA supports families and clinicians choosing treatment for newly diagnosed juvenile idiopathic arthritis.

Front Pediatr 2024

iatgen

A free, widely adopted tool for building and analyzing survey-based Implicit Association Tests in Qualtrics — bringing a lab instrument into ordinary online survey research.

Behav Res Methods 2019

piNET

A web platform for downstream analysis and visualization of proteomics data, linking peptides and post-translational modifications to pathways and networks.

Nucleic Acids Res 2020

GRcalculator

An online tool for calculating and mining growth-rate–corrected dose–response metrics, which correct a long-standing bias in how drug potency is measured.

BMC Cancer 2017
